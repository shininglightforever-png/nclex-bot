💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #273!

💉 One tiny bubble in this syringe is supposed to be there. Do you know why?

This is a HIGH-YIELD pharmacology question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: B — Do not expel the air bubble

🧠 Rationale: Enoxaparin is a low molecular weight heparin.

The air bubble in the prefilled syringe helps make sure the full dose is given.

Do not aspirate or rub the site, since both increase bruising.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Enoxaparin

💊 Drug Class: Low molecular weight heparin

❤️ Primary Use: Prevent and treat DVT and PE

⚠️ Side Effects:

🔹 Bleeding and bruising

🔹 Low platelets

🔹 Injection site pain

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Inject in the abdomen, 2 inches from the navel

✅ Rotate sites

✅ Do not rub the site

✅ Report black stools or unusual bleeding

🚑 Antidote: Protamine sulfate (partial reversal)

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Keep the bubble, skip the rub!

❤️ Left side or right side? This one trips up so many students!

This is a HIGH-YIELD cardiac question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: C — Crackles in the lungs

🧠 Rationale: When the left ventricle fails, blood backs up into the lungs.

This causes crackles, dyspnea and orthopnea.

JVD, ascites and an enlarged liver point to right-sided failure.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Left-Sided Heart Failure

🫁 Where Fluid Goes: Back into the lungs

⚠️ Signs:

🔹 Crackles

🔹 Dyspnea and orthopnea

🔹 Pink frothy sputum (severe)

🔹 Fatigue

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ High Fowler position

✅ Give oxygen as ordered

✅ Give diuretics as ordered

✅ Daily weights and strict I and O

🚑 Escalate: Pink frothy sputum or sudden worsening dyspnea

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Left = Lungs. Right = Rest of the body.

✅ Correct Answer: A — Cold intolerance and weight gain

🧠 Rationale: Hypothyroidism slows the body's metabolism.

Clients feel cold, gain weight and feel tired.

✅ Correct Answer: B — Pain or swelling in the heel

🧠 Rationale: Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic.

These drugs carry a boxed warning for tendinitis and tendon rupture, most often the Achilles tendon.

Heel pain or swelling means stop exercise and call the provider right away.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Ciprofloxacin

💊 Drug Class: Fluoroquinolone antibiotic

❤️ Primary Use: UTIs and other bacterial infections

🔹 Sun sensitivity

🔹 Dizziness

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Report heel or tendon pain right away

✅ Drink plenty of fluids

✅ Take 2 hours before or 6 hours after antacids, iron or calcium

✅ Use sunscreen

🚑 Higher Risk: Older adults, steroid use, kidney, heart or lung transplant

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #270!

🫁 Bubbles in a chest tube can be normal or a problem. Do you know which chamber matters?

This is a HIGH-YIELD respiratory question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: B — An air leak in the system

🧠 Rationale: Continuous bubbling in the water seal chamber means an air leak.

Gentle bubbling in the suction control chamber is expected with wet suction.

Intermittent bubbling in the water seal with coughing or exhaling can be normal with a pneumothorax.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Chest Tubes

🫁 Water Seal Chamber:

🔹 Tidaling up and down with breathing is normal

🔹 Continuous bubbling means an air leak

🔹 Tidaling stops when the lung re-expands or the tube kinks

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Check connections from the client to the drain

✅ Keep the drain below the chest

✅ Keep the tubing free of kinks and loops

✅ Never clamp without an order

🚑 Report: Sudden drainage over 100 mL/hr or new trouble breathing

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Water seal bubbling nonstop? Look for the leak!

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #271!

🤰 Lying flat can cause trouble late in pregnancy. What is the quick fix?

This is a HIGH-YIELD maternity question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: D — Turn the client onto her left side

🧠 Rationale: Lying flat lets the heavy uterus press on the inferior vena cava.

Less blood returns to the heart, so blood pressure drops and the client feels dizzy.

Turning to the left side takes pressure off the vena cava and restores blood flow.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Supine Hypotensive Syndrome

⚠️ Signs:

🔹 Dizziness and lightheadedness

🔹 Pale, clammy skin

🔹 Low blood pressure

🔹 Fast heart rate

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Turn to the left side

✅ Use a wedge under the right hip if the client must be on her back

✅ Recheck vital signs

✅ Monitor the fetal heart rate

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Feeling faint? Go left, it's great!
