✅ Correct Answer: B — Take It with a Full Glass of Plain Water

🧠 Rationale: Alendronate must be taken first thing in the morning on an empty stomach.

A full glass of plain water helps it reach the stomach and lowers the risk of esophagus irritation.

Food, coffee and milk block absorption, and lying down can cause esophagitis.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Alendronate

💊 Drug Class: Bisphosphonate

❤️ Primary Use: Preventing and treating osteoporosis

⚠️ Side Effects:

🔹 Heartburn and esophagus irritation

🔹 Trouble swallowing

🔹 Rare jaw bone problems

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Take in the morning before food or other meds

✅ Use 6 to 8 oz of plain water only

✅ Stay upright for at least 30 minutes

✅ Wait 30 minutes before eating

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #260!

❤️ Chest pain in the ED. Which lab tells you it is a heart attack?

This is a HIGH-YIELD cardiac question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: C — Troponin

🧠 Rationale: Troponin is the most specific lab for damage to heart muscle.

It starts to rise within a few hours of a myocardial infarction and stays high for days.

Potassium, hemoglobin and sodium matter, but they do not confirm an MI.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Troponin and MI

❤️ What It Shows: Injury to the heart muscle

⏱️ Timing: Rises within about 3 to 4 hours and stays high up to 2 weeks

⚠️ MI Signs:

🔹 Chest pain or pressure

🔹 Pain to the jaw, arm or back

🔹 Shortness of breath and sweating

🔹 Women may have fatigue or nausea only

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Get a 12 lead ECG within 10 minutes

✅ Draw serial troponins

✅ Give oxygen if SpO2 is low

✅ Give aspirin and nitroglycerin as ordered

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Troponin tells the truth about the heart!

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #261!

🫁 Suctioning a trach the wrong way can drop oxygen fast. What comes first?

This is a HIGH-YIELD respiratory question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: B — Give 100% Oxygen Before Suctioning

🧠 Rationale: Suctioning removes oxygen along with secretions.

Giving 100% oxygen first helps prevent hypoxia.

Suction is applied only while pulling the catheter out, for 10 to 15 seconds at most.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Tracheostomy Suctioning

🫁 When to Suction: Only when needed, such as noisy breathing, visible secretions or low SpO2

⚠️ Watch For:

🔹 Low oxygen saturation

🔹 Bradycardia or other dysrhythmias

🔹 Airway irritation

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Use sterile technique in the hospital

✅ Hyperoxygenate before and after

✅ Suction 10 to 15 seconds per pass

✅ Rest the client between passes

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Oxygen in, suction out, 15 seconds tops!

✅ Correct Answer: D — 7

🧠 Rationale: A GCS of 8 or less means a severe brain injury.

At this level the client may not be able to protect the airway.

A score of 15 is normal, 13 to 15 is mild and 9 to 12 is moderate.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Glasgow Coma Scale

🧠 What It Measures: Eye opening, verbal response and motor response

📊 Score Range: 3 (lowest) to 15 (normal)

🔹 9 to 12: moderate

🔹 8 or less: severe

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Check GCS often and trend the scores

✅ Report any drop of 2 or more points

✅ Protect the airway in low scores

🚑 Emergency: A falling GCS can mean rising ICP. Notify the provider right away.

Less than 8, intubate!

✅ Correct Answer: D — Bananas

🧠 Rationale: Bananas are high in potassium.

Damaged kidneys cannot remove extra potassium well.

High potassium can cause dangerous heart rhythms.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Chronic Kidney Disease Diet

🫘 What Happens: The kidneys cannot filter waste, fluid and electrolytes well

🔹 High sodium: canned and salty foods

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Avoid salt substitutes, which often contain potassium

✅ Take phosphate binders with meals

🚑 Emergency: Muscle weakness or an irregular heartbeat can mean high potassium.

Bad kidneys, no bananas!

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #252!

💉 Can IV potassium ever be pushed?

This is a HIGH-YIELD pharmacology question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: B — Dilute It and Infuse with a Pump

🧠 Rationale: IV potassium is never given by IV push.

A fast dose can cause deadly dysrhythmias and cardiac arrest.

It must be diluted and run slowly on an infusion pump.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: IV Potassium Chloride

💊 Drug Class: Electrolyte replacement

❤️ Primary Use: Treating low potassium (normal 3.5 to 5.0 mEq/L)

⚠️ Watch For:

🔹 Burning or pain at the IV site

🔹 Peaked T waves if the level goes too high

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Always use an infusion pump

✅ Check kidney function and urine output first

✅ Keep the client on a cardiac monitor for higher rates

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Potassium: never push, always pump!

🧬 Low cortisol can change the color of the skin. Do you know how?

This is a HIGH-YIELD endocrine question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

✅ Correct Answer: A — Bronze or Darkened Skin

🧠 Rationale: Addison disease is adrenal insufficiency: too little cortisol and aldosterone.

High ACTH levels darken the skin, giving a bronze look.

Moon face and weight gain point to Cushing syndrome instead.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Addison Disease

❤️ What Happens: The adrenal glands make too little cortisol and aldosterone

⚠️ Signs:

🔹 Bronze skin

🔹 Weight loss and fatigue

🔹 Low sodium, high potassium, low glucose

🔹 Low blood pressure

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Teach lifelong steroid replacement

✅ Never stop steroids suddenly

✅ Extra doses may be needed during illness or stress

🚑 Emergency: Addisonian crisis causes severe low BP and shock. Expect IV fluids and IV hydrocortisone.

Addison = Adding bronze to the skin, losing sodium and weight!

✅ Correct Answer: A — Heart Rate 52 Beats per Minute

🧠 Rationale: Metoprolol is a beta blocker that lowers heart rate and blood pressure.

A heart rate below 60 usually means hold the dose and call the provider.

The other vital signs are within normal limits.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Metoprolol

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Never stop the drug suddenly

✅ Correct Answer: A — Trachea Shifted Away from the Injured Side

🧠 Rationale: In tension pneumothorax, trapped air builds pressure in the chest.

That pressure pushes the trachea and heart toward the unaffected side.

✅ Give high flow oxygen

✅ Prepare for needle decompression and a chest tube

🚑 Emergency: This can cause cardiac arrest within minutes.

🩸 Chills and back pain during a transfusion. What comes FIRST?

This is a HIGH-YIELD safety question every nursing student should know! 🩺

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: A — Stop the Transfusion

🧠 Rationale: Chills, fever and low back pain suggest an acute transfusion reaction.

Stopping the blood right away limits how much enters the body.

Then keep the line open with normal saline and notify the provider and blood bank.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Blood Transfusion Reactions

⚠️ Signs:

🔹 Low back pain

🔹 Hives or itching

🔹 Shortness of breath or low BP

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Stop the transfusion

✅ Change the tubing and run normal saline

✅ Stay with the client and monitor vitals

✅ Send the blood bag back to the blood bank

⏱️ Key Fact: Most reactions happen in the first 15 minutes, so stay with the client
