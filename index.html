✅ Correct Answer: A — Stop the Infusion and Notify the Provider

🧠 Rationale: This is a vancomycin infusion reaction (often called red man syndrome or vancomycin flushing syndrome).

It is caused by histamine release when vancomycin is infused too fast.

Stop the infusion first, then notify the provider. The provider may order an antihistamine and restart the drug at a slower rate.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Vancomycin

💊 Drug Class: Glycopeptide antibiotic

🦠 Primary Use: Serious gram-positive infections including MRSA and C. difficile (oral)

⚠️ Watch For:

🔹 Flushing and rash of the face, neck and upper body

🔹 Ototoxicity (ringing in the ears, hearing loss)

🔹 Nephrotoxicity (rising creatinine, low urine output)

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Infuse slowly, usually over at least 60 minutes

✅ Monitor trough levels as ordered

✅ Monitor kidney function and hearing

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Vancomycin too fast = flushed face. Stop, notify, slow it down!

⚡ Potassium 6.8 and peaked T waves. What is your FIRST move?

This is a HIGH-YIELD electrolyte question every nursing student should know! 🩺

✅ Correct Answer: B — Place the Client on Continuous Cardiac Monitoring

🧠 Rationale: A normal potassium level is about 3.5 to 5.0 mEq/L.

Severe hyperkalemia can cause dangerous dysrhythmias and cardiac arrest.

Peaked T waves show the heart is already affected, so continuous monitoring comes first while treatment is started.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Hyperkalemia

⚠️ Signs and Symptoms:

🔹 Peaked T waves and dysrhythmias

🔹 Muscle weakness

🔹 Paresthesias

🔹 Nausea and diarrhea

💉 Common Treatments:

✅ IV calcium gluconate to protect the heart

✅ IV regular insulin with dextrose to shift potassium into cells

✅ Sodium polystyrene sulfonate or other binders to remove potassium

✅ Dialysis for severe cases or kidney failure

🚨 Common Causes:

✅ Kidney failure

✅ Potassium-sparing diuretics and ACE inhibitors

✅ Tissue damage such as burns or crush injuries

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

High K = Heart at risk. Monitor first!

🧠 Expected side effect or lithium toxicity? Can you tell the difference?

✅ Correct Answer: C — Coarse Tremor and Persistent Vomiting

🧠 Rationale: Lithium has a narrow therapeutic range, usually about 0.6 to 1.2 mEq/L for maintenance.

A fine hand tremor, thirst and mild nausea are common expected effects.

A coarse tremor, persistent vomiting or diarrhea, and confusion point to toxicity. Hold the dose and notify the provider.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Lithium

💊 Drug Class: Mood stabilizer

🧠 Primary Use: Bipolar disorder

⚠️ Signs of Toxicity:

🔹 Coarse tremor

🔹 Persistent vomiting and diarrhea

🔹 Confusion and slurred speech

🔹 Muscle weakness and poor coordination

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Drink 2 to 3 L of fluid daily

✅ Keep sodium intake steady. Low sodium raises lithium levels

✅ Avoid NSAIDs and get regular blood levels checked

✅ Report vomiting, diarrhea or heavy sweating since dehydration raises levels

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Fine tremor = fine. Coarse tremor = call!

✅ Correct Answer: B — Wash Hands with Soap and Water

🧠 Rationale: C. difficile forms spores.

Alcohol-based hand rubs do not kill these spores.

Soap, water and friction physically remove the spores from your hands.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: C. difficile

🦠 What It Is: A spore-forming bacteria that causes severe diarrhea and colitis

⚠️ Risk Factors:

🔹 Recent antibiotic use

🔹 Older adults

🔹 Long hospital or nursing home stays

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Contact precautions with gown and gloves

✅ Soap and water hand hygiene

✅ Dedicated equipment such as a stethoscope for the room

✅ Clean the room with a bleach-based disinfectant

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

C. diff = spores. Spores beat sanitizer. Soap and water wins!

🩸 Heparin is running and the client is bleeding. Which antidote do you grab?

This is a HIGH-YIELD pharmacology question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

✅ Correct Answer: B — Protamine Sulfate

🧠 Rationale: Protamine sulfate is the reversal agent for heparin.

Vitamin K reverses warfarin, not heparin.

Naloxone reverses opioids and flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Heparin

💊 Drug Class: Anticoagulant

❤️ Primary Use: Prevent and treat clots such as DVT and PE

🧪 Lab to Monitor: aPTT

⚠️ Watch For:

🔹 Bleeding gums, nosebleeds and blood in urine or stool

🔹 Bruising

🔹 Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (dropping platelets)

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Stop the infusion if bleeding occurs and notify the provider

✅ Use an infusion pump and double-check doses

✅ Use a soft toothbrush and electric razor

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Heparin = Protamine. Warfarin = Vitamin K!

💊 NCLEX PRACTICE QUESTION #244!

🌬️ Why does pursed-lip breathing actually help clients with COPD?

This is a HIGH-YIELD respiratory question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.
.
.
.
.
.
.
.

✅ Correct Answer: B — It Keeps Airways Open Longer During Exhalation

🧠 Rationale: In COPD, small airways collapse early during exhalation and trap air in the lungs.

Pursed-lip breathing creates back pressure that holds those airways open.

This helps the client push out more carbon dioxide and reduces shortness of breath.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: COPD Breathing

🫁 How to Teach It:

✅ Breathe in through the nose for about 2 counts

✅ Purse the lips like blowing out a candle

✅ Breathe out slowly for about 4 counts

⚠️ COPD Signs to Know:

🔹 Barrel chest

🔹 Use of accessory muscles

🔹 Chronic cough and sputum

🔹 Tripod positioning

🚨 Nursing Tips:

✅ High Fowler or tripod position helps breathing

✅ Give oxygen as prescribed and watch for CO2 retention

✅ Encourage small frequent meals to reduce fatigue

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Smell the roses, blow out the candle!

🚨 Drooling, high fever and tripod position in a child. What should you NOT do?

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.

.

.

✅ Correct Answer: B — Inspect the Throat with a Tongue Depressor

🧠 Rationale: Epiglottitis is a medical emergency because the swollen epiglottis can block the airway.

Putting anything in the throat can trigger a spasm and complete airway obstruction.

Keep the child calm, keep emergency airway equipment close and let the provider examine the throat in a controlled setting.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Epiglottitis

⚠️ The 4 Ds:

🔹 Drooling

🔹 Dysphagia (trouble swallowing)

🔹 Dysphonia (muffled voice)

🔹 Distressed breathing

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Do NOT use a tongue depressor or throat swab

✅ Keep the child upright and calm, often on the parent's lap

✅ Have intubation and tracheostomy equipment at the bedside

✅ Avoid upsetting procedures that make the child cry

🛡️ Prevention: The Hib vaccine has made epiglottitis much less common

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Drooling + tripod = Do NOT touch the throat!
