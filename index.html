This is a HIGH-YIELD pharmacology question every nursing student should know! 🩺

✅ Correct Answer: B — Keep vitamin K intake consistent

🧠 Rationale: Vitamin K reverses the effect of warfarin.

Clients do not need to avoid greens. Big changes in intake make the INR swing.

Steady, consistent intake keeps the dose working the same way each day.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Warfarin (Coumadin)

💊 Drug Class:

🔹 Oral anticoagulant (vitamin K antagonist)

📊 Lab to Monitor:

🔹 PT/INR, usual goal 2 to 3 for most clients

⚠️ Signs of Bleeding:

🔹 Bleeding gums or nosebleeds

🔹 Black, tarry stools

🔹 Blood in urine

🔹 Easy bruising

🚨 Patient Teaching:

✅ Keep vitamin K foods steady

✅ Use a soft toothbrush and electric razor

✅ Avoid aspirin and NSAIDs unless approved

✅ Wear a medical alert ID

🚑 Antidote:

🔹 Vitamin K (phytonadione)

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

✅ Correct Answer: C — ST-segment elevation

🧠 Rationale: Read the test in 48 to 72 hours.

🩸 High sugar or low sugar? Can you tell them apart?

This is a HIGH-YIELD endocrine question every nursing student should know! 🩺

👇 Comment your answer before checking!

.

✅ Correct Answer: A — Increased thirst and urination

🧠 Rationale: High glucose spills into the urine and pulls water with it.

This causes polyuria, then thirst (polydipsia).

Shakiness, sweating, clammy skin and hunger point to hypoglycemia.

📚 NCLEX Must-Know: Hyperglycemia vs Hypoglycemia

📈 Hyperglycemia Signs:

🔹 Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia

🔹 Hot, dry skin

🔹 Blurred vision and fatigue

📉 Hypoglycemia Signs:

🔹 Shaky, sweaty, cool and clammy

🔹 Hunger and anxiety

🔹 Confusion if severe

🚨 Nursing Actions:

✅ Check glucose first

✅ Low and awake: 15 g fast carbs, recheck in 15 min

✅ High: follow insulin orders, check ketones

🚑 When to Escalate:

🔹 Unconscious client: glucagon or IV dextrose

💡 NCLEX MEMORY TIP:

Hot and dry, sugar high. Cold and clammy, need some candy!

✅ Stay upright 30 minutes after meals

🧠 Rationale: Regular insulin is clear. NPH is cloudy.

✅ Roll NPH gently, do not shake

✅ Never mix glargine with other insulins

🧠 Rationale: In COPD, small airways collapse early during exhalation and trap air in the lungs.

🫁 How to Teach It:

✅ Breathe in through the nose for about 2 counts

✅ Purse the lips like blowing out a candle

✅ Breathe out slowly for about 4 counts

🔹 Barrel chest

🔹 Use of accessory muscles

🔹 Chronic cough and sputum

🔹 Tripod positioning

🚨 Nursing Tips:

✅ High Fowler or tripod position helps breathing

✅ Give oxygen as prescribed and watch for CO2 retention

✅ Encourage small frequent meals to reduce fatigue

🚨 Nursing Actions:
